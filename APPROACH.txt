AQUAMESH - APPROACH AND DESIGN RATIONALE
=========================================
A temporal, multi-sensor, explainable reasoning system for a miniature flood model
(river / drain / land), running entirely on the Raspberry Pi.

Read this first (5 minutes), then run the demo (README.txt).


1. WHAT PROBLEM THE SOFTWARE SOLVES
-----------------------------------
Your hardware produces evidence from several imperfect sources:

  FLOOD01 (ESP32 + LoRa)   RAIN, SOIL, HX (load cell)      every 5 s
  GAS01   (ESP32 + LoRa)   MQ gas, DIST (MPU6050 fin motion) every 5 s
  Pi camera (waterwatch)   river / drain / land water coverage, motion, level
  TTGO gateway             delivers the LoRa packets to the Pi over USB serial

A single threshold ("rain > 500 -> ALERT") cannot tell the difference between:
  - heavy rain overloading a healthy drain   vs   a blocked drain,
  - a real surge   vs   a stuck or spoofed sensor,
  - "all clear"    vs   "I cannot see anything because a node died".

So the system is not a threshold alarm. It builds a picture of the world state over time,
infers events that nobody measures directly (surge, blockage, inundation), checks how those
events propagate to other zones, and explains every conclusion with an evidence path.

4. HOW A MEASUREMENT BECOMES A CONCLUSION (worked example: compound scenario)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 t=10-40 s  RAIN rises.                    event rain_high ............ (rain sensor)
 t~40 s     soil moisture climbs.          event soil_rising ......... (soil sensor)
            river coverage + flow rise,
            MPU motion above baseline.     events river_cover_rising, river_flow_surging,
                                           river_motion_up .......... (camera, MPU)
            R01: rain_high AND soil_rising           => runoff_potential_high
            R03: river_cover_rising AND corroboration => river_surge_suspected
            R04: surge AND river>drain edge open AND drain rising => drain_backflow_risk
            S02: => RIVER WARNING
 t=110 s    drain obstructed. Level rises, throughput collapses.
            R05/R05b: drain level up (or high) AND flow falling/stalled => drain_blockage_suspected
            R08: surge AND blockage                  => compound_event
            S05: blockage AND drain full             => DRAIN CRITICAL
 t~200 s    water spills.
            R10: drain full AND drain>land edge open AND (blockage | saturated soil) => land risk
            R09: land wet area expanding (direct observation, fast path)
            S08/S09                                  => LAND WARNING -> CRITICAL
Click LAND in the dashboard: the backward-chaining panel lists every satisfied rule and each
sensor fact that supports it, e.g. "standing water on land is expanding (+3.8%/min, z=11.8) [camera];
drain is nearly full [camera]; soil near saturation [soil sensor]; ... Independent evidence
families: MPU, camera, rain sensor, soil sensor (4)."


5. SCIENTIFIC GROUNDING OF EACH LAYER
-------------------------------------
5.1 Vision features (vision.py; your waterwatch method)
  - Wetness = deviation of the smoothed LAB colour from a learned DRY baseline, in units of that
    pixel's own standard deviation (K = 3.5 sigma), persisting >= 3 frames. A per-pixel z-test.
  - Surface motion = Farneback dense optical flow (Farneback 2003), thresholded per pixel against
    the dry-baseline peak motion.
  - Still water = wet AND not moving; moving water = flow magnitude above threshold.
  - Drain level = fraction of the channel's length (PCA axis, 20 bins) that is wet: a top-down view
    cannot see depth, so this is length-fill, not depth. Stated limitation.
  - Added for this project: river "cover" (wet fraction of the river region) and drain "flow"
    (moving fraction x mean motion), so the reasoner can compare level vs throughput.
  - The camera is auto-paused when >50% of the region changes (hand in view / lighting change) and
    warns if the camera moved (phase correlation). Both become health conditions, not false floods.

5.2 Trend detection (features.py)
  Over a sliding window (60-90 s) fit an OLS line; accept a trend only if
        z = slope / SE(slope)  >  2.5   AND  |slope| > a physical minimum
  (e.g. river coverage > +6 %/min). This separates a real rise from sensor noise: a noisy flat
  signal has a large SE, a clean ramp has a tiny one. Events then pass through persistence +
  hysteresis latches so they do not flicker.
  Calm baselines (river motion, MPU DIST) are EWMA mean/variance learned only while calm and
  frozen while elevated, so a sustained surge is not "learned away" (a bug found and fixed
  during testing).

5.2b Calibrated inputs
  RAIN and SOIL raw ADC are normalised to 0..1 between a "dry" and "wet" reference. ASSUMPTION:
  higher raw = wetter (consistent with your node's ALERT_RAW). Use the dashboard buttons
  "rain = dry / wet", "soil = dry / wet" to calibrate from the live reading. If your soil sensor
  reads LOWER when wetter, calibrate dry = high reading and wet = low reading; the maths handles it.

5.3 Rules (kb.py) - starter rules, not validated hydrology
  R01 rain_high AND (soil_saturated OR soil_rising)            -> runoff_potential_high
  R02 rain_high AND soil still absorbing                        -> runoff_potential_moderate
  R03 river_cover_rising AND (flow surge | MPU | runoff | rain) -> river_surge_suspected
  R03b river_flow_surging AND (MPU | rain | runoff)             -> river_surge_suspected (plateau-proof)
  R04 surge AND edge river>drain open AND drain rising          -> drain_backflow_risk
  R05 drain level rising AND flow falling/stalled               -> drain_blockage_suspected
  R05b drain nearly full AND flow falling/stalled               -> drain_blockage_suspected
  R06 before(rain_high, drain_level_rising, 900 s)              -> drain_rise_follows_rain
  R07 level rising AND flow rising AND follows rain             -> drain_loading_from_rain (normal)
  R07b loading AND drain nearly full                            -> drain_overload_suspected
  R08 surge AND blockage                                        -> compound_event
  R09 land wet area expanding                                   -> land_inundation_risk_rising (fast path)
  R10 drain full AND drain>land edge open AND (blockage|overload|saturated|land wet) -> land risk
  R11 surge AND river>land edge open AND soil saturated         -> land risk (overbank)
  S01-S09 map hypotheses to WATCH / WARNING / CRITICAL per zone.
  Thresholds live in aquamesh/config.py ("th"). Tune them with your own runs.

5.4 Bayesian network (bn.py)
  Hidden causes: Rain, SoilSat, Surge, Block (drain obstruction), Inund (land inundation).
  Structure (causal): Rain -> SoilSat, Rain -> Surge, {Block, Surge, Rain, SoilSat} -> Inund.
  Evidence nodes are children of the causes (RainHigh, SoilRising, HxRising, SoilSatObs,
  RiverCoverRising, RiverFlowUp, RiverMotionUp, DrainLevelRising, DrainLevelHigh,
  DrainFlowFalling, DrainFlowRising, LandWetExpanding).
  - Inference: exact enumeration over the 2^5 hidden states (32) - trivial on a Pi.
  - Multi-cause effects (drain level, inundation) use noisy-OR (Pearl 1988): each active cause
    independently fails to produce the effect with probability (1 - w).
  - Topology gating: a closed terrain edge removes that cause from the noisy-OR. The elevation
    experiment therefore changes both the rules AND the probabilities.
  - Source reliability r in [0,1] enters as TEMPERED virtual evidence: likelihood = P(obs|cause)^r.
    r = 0 ignores the source; stale or suspect sources get r = 0.5.
  - Common-cause discount: k evidence items from the same sensor are not independent, so each is
    exponentiated by k^-0.25 (heuristic) to reduce double counting.
  - Saturation censoring: when the drain is full, "level not rising" is uninformative and is dropped.
  - Leave-one-out evidence weights (change in log-odds when one item is removed) are shown on the
    dashboard, which also supports the ablation argument.
  IMPORTANT: all CPT numbers are EXPERT PRIORS for a miniature model. They are hypotheses to be
  calibrated against your recorded experiments (section 7), not measured probabilities.

5.5 Risk, confidence and "never silently safe"
  Severity (rules) and probability (BN) are shown side by side with an agreement indicator.
  Confidence is separate from severity:
        confidence = coverage x independence
        coverage     = sum over the zone's expected sources of weight x (reliability / nominal)
        independence = 1.0 if >= 2 independent evidence families support the call, else 0.75
  If coverage < 0.9 the zone shows DEGRADED; < 0.3 and no risk evidence -> UNKNOWN (never "NORMAL").
  Example: "HIGH risk, LOW confidence, soil sensor offline" is representable and displayed.

5.6 Physical caveats (stated up front, shown on the dashboard)
  - MPU6050 is NOT a flow meter. DIST is a relative vibration score; it is used only as a
    SECONDARY corroborating signal (reliability 0.5) for river surge. Velocity from integrating an
    accelerometer drifts. Camera motion is the primary flow signal and is relative, not m/s.
  - Q ~ v x A needs a cross-section. Everything here is RELATIVE (coverage, level fraction,
    motion index), which is appropriate for a miniature demonstration.
  - Gas (MQ): logged and graphed, NOT used for inference ("gas_in_inference" is false), per your
    own caution that a gas trend cannot by itself prove a water-level change. Include it only after
    controlled experiments show a repeatable relationship.
  - HX (load cell): assumed to measure catchment/bucket weight (rising = water collected). Used as
    weak corroboration of rain only. Change/remove if your mounting differs.
  - The camera does not measure elevation. The elevation experiment edits the terrain model
    (dashboard) - do not claim the camera inferred it.


6. EVIDENCE INTEGRITY, FAULTS AND MANIPULATION
----------------------------------------------
Implemented (evidence.py, protocol.py, health.py):
  - Per-packet identity: node, PKT sequence, UP (node uptime) and gateway receive time kept separately.
  - CRC-8 verification (same polynomial as the firmware). Corrupted or naively altered packets
    are rejected and logged.
  - Session tracking: lost packets (sequence gaps), duplicates, reboots (PKT restarts with small UP),
    and REPLAY / DELAY detection: UP must advance in step with the gateway clock; a packet whose
    UP is >20 s inconsistent, or an old PKT, is rejected as stale/replayed (alert raised).
  - Physical plausibility: ADC rail pinning, stuck values, single-sample glitches (median-of-3),
    HX711-not-ready, MPU error, MQ warm-up; cross-sensor test: sustained rain with no response
    from soil/river/drain/load cell -> rain reliability halved and flagged "uncorroborated".
  - Tamper-evident log: every record (packets, rejected packets, features, rule firings,
    decisions, alerts, audits, calibrations, terrain edits) is stored in SQLite with
        hash_i = SHA-256( hash_{i-1} | id | time | kind | payload )
    so editing, deleting or reordering any record breaks every later hash. An independent checkpoint
    (id + hash) is appended and fsync'ed to a side file every 100 records. "Verify chain" and the
    "Tamper demo" button (edits a COPY of the log and shows detection) are on the dashboard;
    tools/verify_log.py does it offline.
  - Random audit: at unpredictable times (60-180 s apart, system RNG) a random 60 s window is
    re-checked: CRCs recomputed from the stored raw packets, calibrated features recomputed and
    compared with what the decision engine used, and (live mode) a camera frame is saved with its
    hash. Mismatches are logged as failed audits.
  - Local-first: all inference and alerts run on the Pi. Alerts are also queued in an SQLite outbox
    and pushed to PUSH_URL when reachable; internet loss only delays sync.

Honest limits (please state these in the demo):
  - CRC-8 detects accidental corruption; it does NOT authenticate the sender. An attacker who
    recomputes the CRC can inject a value. In the "replay + spoof" scenario the spoofed value is
    caught only by CROSS-SENSOR CONSISTENCY, not by cryptography. The correct upgrade is a
    per-node truncated HMAC-SHA-256 with a counter (cf. RFC 8613 replay protection); it needs
    firmware changes on all nodes and the TTGO. Not implemented.
  - The hash chain makes edits DETECTABLE after the fact; it cannot stop an attacker who controls
    the Pi, the gateway, a node's firmware or its keys. Keep the checkpoint file (or its latest
    line) somewhere else (e.g. email it) to make rewriting the whole chain detectable.
  - Writes are batched (flushed each second) to limit microSD wear.

Two qualifications from your plan that the software respects:
  - A single LoRa star does not re-route around a failed node; the software therefore tests failure
    DETECTION and stale-data handling, not rerouting (no mesh forwarding exists).
  - Gateway loss with one gateway cannot deliver new readings; the software reports a degraded
    network state instead of pretending otherwise.

9. WHAT IS NOT DONE (and the sensible next steps)
-------------------------------------------------
  - Neural network / learned model: deliberately deferred until you have labelled runs. The evidence
    log already stores features + decisions so datasets can be exported (CSV, SQLite).
  - Calibrating thresholds and the BN's probabilities from your recorded experiments (e.g. fit
    likelihoods from labelled windows). This is the main scientific next step.
  - HMAC-authenticated packets (firmware change), mesh/forwarding for rerouting, weather-forecast feed.
  - Automatic vision-vs-reference disagreement scoring.
  - The vision code is your waterwatch logic, extended and refactored but NOT run on a real Pi camera
    in my environment (it was smoke-tested on synthetic frames). Expect to re-tune K_SIGMA, PROC_W/H
    and the Pi 3B frame rate (4-6 fps at 320x240).
  - The sim's dynamics are simple first-order ODEs chosen to be plausible and to exercise the logic.
  - Live-mode serial parsing was tested on text lines in your documented format (including optional
    RSSI/SNR suffix), not on a real TTGO. Check the first lines in the Evidence panel.
  - The sample line in your notes has "C=3F" as a placeholder; real CRCs are computed by your firmware.
    (For FROM=FLOOD01,PKT=33,STATUS=NORMAL,RAIN=120,SOIL=330,HX=192547,UP=165,FW=2.1 the true CRC is F3.)


10. REFERENCES (for the methods used)
-------------------------------------
  - Pearl, J. (1988). Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems (Bayesian networks, noisy-OR).
  - Russell & Norvig. Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach (production systems, forward and
    backward chaining, abduction as inference to the best explanation).
  - CLIPS expert-system tool (clipsrules.net): fact/rule matching model that kb.py follows in spirit.
  - Allen, J. (1983). Maintaining knowledge about temporal intervals (the "before" relation).
  - Farneback, G. (2003). Two-frame motion estimation based on polynomial expansion (optical flow).
  - Haber & Stornetta (1991) and Schneier & Kelsey (1999): hash-chained timestamping / secure audit logs.
  - RFC 8613 (OSCORE): sequence numbers + authenticated messages for replay protection (design reference
    for the HMAC upgrade).
  - Multi-sensor / multi-temporal Bayesian evidence fusion for flood monitoring (literature cited in
    your plan, e.g. the work indexed at iris.cnr.it).
